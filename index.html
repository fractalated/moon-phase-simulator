    It isn’t Earth’s shadow

    Half the Moon is always lit. A phase is just how much of that lit half is turned toward us. Earth’s shadow only touches the Moon during a lunar eclipse — rare, because the orbit is tilted about 5° and usually misses.

    There is no dark side

    The same face points at us always, but sunlight sweeps across it: every spot on the Moon gets roughly 14.8 days of day and 14.8 days of night. The far side is unseen, not unlit.

    Daytime moons are normal

    Only the full moon rises at sunset and sets at sunrise. A first quarter moon rises around noon and is easy to spot in an afternoon sky — the Moon rises about 50 minutes later each day.
